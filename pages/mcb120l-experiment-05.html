UC DAVIS / MCB120L / EXPERIMENT 05

Protein Purification
Finding the target protein in a mixture

Capture GAF-4 with a chitin affinity column and fractionate LDH with ammonium sulfate. Track purification using spectra, enzyme activity, and protein concentration.

Based on Experiment 5 Manual_W24 · Introductory study notes

Purpose · Why purify proteins?

Cells contain many proteins. To study one, separate it from other components while preserving function. Two approaches ask:Where is the target, how much remains, and is it purer?

01 · GAF-4

Express a tagged cyanobacterial photosensory domain in E. coli, then capture and release it with chitin affinity chromatography.

02 · LDH

Start with bovine-muscle crude extract and separate native lactate dehydrogenase at different ammonium-sulfate saturations.

03 · Verify

Spectra track chromophore, A340 tracks catalysis, and Bradford measures total protein. Compare recovery and purification fold.

Key point 1 · Concentration, purification, and recovery differ

Concentration: Move protein into a smaller volume to increase concentration. Purification: Remove other proteins to increase the target's proportion. Recovery: How much starting target remains? Higher concentration need not mean higher purity, and strong purification may lose substantial target.

GAF-4 ammonium-sulfate treatment mainly precipitates and concentrates before selective affinity chromatography. LDH 40%→60% fractionation separates proteins by solubility differences.

Route A · GAF-4 / Intein-CBD affinity purification

GAF-4 comes from Nostoc punctiforme NpR6012 and binds the phycocyanobilin (PCB) chromophore. In the fusion, CBD(Chitin Binding Domain) binds chitin; Intein undergoes DTT-induced cleavage, releasing GAF-4 while the tag remains on the column.

Expression background · Roles of arabinose and IPTG

Staff precultured XJb(DE) E. coli carrying pBAD/GAF-Intein-CBD. Arabinose regulates pBAD through AraC; a second plasmid supplies IPTG-induced PCB biosynthesis. The medium contains 3.0 mM arabinose, 1.0 mM IPTG, 1.0 mM MgCl₂, 50 μg/mL ampicillin, and 25 μg/mL kanamycin; culture at room temperature for 16–20 hours. These are staff preparations, not student-completed steps.

Day 1 · Lyse, concentrate, load

  1. Thaw for lysis: Staff resuspend cells and freeze at −80°C for 30 minutes; students thaw completely. Add 60 μL 100X DNase I buffer + 80 μL DNase I (10 U/μL), mix, and incubate at 37°C for 30 minutes. DNase degrades DNA to reduce viscosity.
  2. Clarify: Add 4 mL 20 mM HEPES pH 8.0 to approximately 6 mL lysate. Centrifuge in SS-34 at 4°C, 10,000 rpm for 10 minutes. Retain the supernatant as the post-DNase I fraction; measure volume and save 300 μL.
  3. Ammonium-sulfate concentration: Add 14.4 mL of 95%-saturated ammonium sulfate to approximately 9 mL remaining supernatant. Gently invert, incubate on ice for 30 minutes, and centrifuge identically. Retain the pellet. The manual specifies the full salt volume even with less than 9 mL supernatant; then final saturation is not exactly 60%.
  4. Resuspend: Dissolve the blue pellet in 500 μL 20 mM HEPES pH 8.0, then add 5 mL Column Buffer. Measure volume and save 300 μL.
  5. Load and wash: Slowly load an equilibrated chitin column. Wash the 0.5 mL resin bed with 5 mL Column Buffer: 10 column volumes. Do not let the resin bed dry.
  6. Induce cleavage: Add 1.5 mL fresh Cleavage Buffer with 30 mM DTT: three column volumes. Close while liquid remains above the resin, cover, label, and refrigerate until the next class.

Column Buffer: 20 mM HEPES pH 8.0, 0.1 mM EDTA, 500 mM NaCl, 0.1% Triton X-100. Cleavage and Elution Buffers use the same HEPES, EDTA, and NaCl; cleavage buffer adds DTT and elution buffer does not.

Example · Why approximately 60% saturation?

Nominal liquid-mixing approximation: Sfinal ≈ (S₁V₁ + S₂V₂)/(V₁+V₂). Assume no initial ammonium sulfate:

95% × 14.4 / (9 + 14.4) ≈ 58.5%

Hence the manual's “nominally 60%.” This is an approximation for mixing prepared salt solutions, not adding solid ammonium sulfate. Temperature, volume changes, and initial salts affect exact saturation.

Day 2 · Elute and evaluate

  1. Add 1.5 mL Elution Buffer, collect approximately 2 mL post-DTT eluate, measure actual volume, and retain all.
  2. For each saved fraction, mix 25 μL sample + 25 μL 2X SDS-PAGE sample buffer, final 1X. Heat at 100°C for 2 minutes and refrigerate for Experiment 6. Heat only electrophoresis aliquots, not samples reserved for functional assays.
  3. Use water baseline and scan 400–800 nm. Illuminate with green and red light for 30 seconds each and record spectra, focusing on 652, 538, and 750 nm. Follow course light-source guidance and avoid eye exposure.
  4. Use Experiment 2 Bradford assays for total protein and combine spectra with later SDS-PAGE to assess enrichment.

750 nm can serve as a background/scattering reference. Example: after green light, A652 = 0.090 and A750 = 0.030 gives corrected signal 0.060. Compare fractions at identical path length, illumination state, and background treatment. For green-minus-red difference spectra, positive 652 nm means stronger signal after green light; state the subtraction order.

Color is not absolute purity

Blue color and photoswitching support chromophore-containing protein. Bradford measures all proteins, and spectra miss chromophore-free GAF-4. PCB molarity from c = A/(εl) requires a reliable coefficient for the state, wavelength, and conditions; the manual provides none to apply directly. No coefficient is invented here; electrophoresis follows in Experiment 6.

Route B · Ammonium-sulfate fractionation of bovine LDH

CE denotes crude extract, S denotes supernatant, P and denotes pellet. The 40–60% LDH cut is material precipitating when 40% supernatant is brought to 60%, not the 60% supernatant.

Day 3 · Fractionation

  1. Staff prepare CE in cold potassium-phosphate buffer. Take 600 μL each into CE, 40a, and 40b tubes. Keep subsequent samples on ice.
  2. Add 400 μL saturated ammonium sulfate to both 40a and 40b: nominal saturation 400/(600+400) = 40%. Invert, incubate on ice for 30 minutes, and microcentrifuge at maximum setting for 10 minutes. Retain 40S and 40P separately.
  3. Add 500 μL saturated ammonium sulfate to 40Sb, incubate on ice for 30 minutes, and centrifuge. For approximately 1,000 μL original supernatant: (0.40×1000 + 500)/1500 = 60%. Retain 60S and 60P separately.
  4. Resuspend one 40P and 60P each in 600 μL cold Tris buffer. Assay CE, 40S, 60S, 40P, and 60P for LDH and Bradford.

600, 1,000, and 1,500 μL are nominal volumes; use actual measured fraction volumes for totals. LDH is expected to remain mainly in 40S then enrich in 60P, but activity must confirm this.

Key point 2 · Why does LDH decrease A340?

Pyruvate + NADH + H⁺ → Lactate + NAD⁺

LDH reduces pyruvate to lactate while consuming NADH. NADH absorbs strongly at 340 nm, while NAD⁺ absorption there is approximately zero, so the rate of A340 decrease gives reaction rate. Absorbance slope is negative, but report positive consumption-based activity.

Example · Premix and initial NADH absorbance

For twelve cocktails: 10.80 mL Tris + 420 μL pyruvate + 420 μL NADH = 11.64 mL. Dispense 970 μL and add enzyme separately. Scale all components equally for excess.

At 1 cm path length, theoretical initial NADH A340 = 6220 × 0.000231 × 1 ≈ 1.44. This comes from nominal concentration, not measured blank or result; 0.04 mM NADH gives approximately 0.249. Use a 340 nm-compatible cuvette and actual path length.

Key point 3 · From A340 to total activity

Uassay = r × Vreaction(mL) × 1000 / (6220 × l(cm))

r is background-corrected NADH consumption in A/min. ε340 = 6220 M⁻¹cm⁻¹; 1 U consumes 1 μmol NADH/min. Use Beer–Lambert, then reaction volume and unit conversion. Convert A/sec to A/min by multiplying by 60.

Original fraction U/mL = Uassay × D / Vsample(mL)

D is the dilution factor before adding sample to the reaction (1/100 → D=100). Here Vsample=0.030 mL. Dividing by sample volume already accounts for sampling; do not multiply again by 1.00/0.030.

Example · LDH activity of 60P

Hypothetical data: resuspended 60P = 0.600 mL; assay a 1/100 dilution in 1.00 mL at 1 cm path length. Replicate consumption rates are 0.120 and 0.124 A/min; matched nonenzymatic background = 0.002 A/min (supplemental control example).

  1. Mean raw rate = (0.120+0.124)/2 = 0.122; corrected r = 0.120 A/min.
  2. Uassay = 0.120×1.00×1000/6220 = 0.0193 U.
  3. Stock activity concentration = 0.0193×100/0.030 = 64.3 U/mL.
  4. 60P total activity = 64.3×0.600 = 38.6 U.
  5. If Bradford stock total protein = 0.800 mg/mL, total protein = 0.800×0.600 = 0.480 mg; specific activity = 38.6/0.480 = 80.4 U/mg.

Valid assay · Linear and proportional to enzyme

If a 1/100 dilution gives corrected 0.120 A/min, a 1/200 dilution should give approximately 0.060 A/min. Use replicates at each dilution and confirm an initially linear time interval. Too-concentrated samples deplete substrate; too-dilute samples approach noise.

A reversed relationship in the manual

The sentence before Part K reverses 1/4 versus 1/2 rates. A 1/2 dilution has twice the enzyme of 1/4 and should give approximately twice the rate. The later 1/8→0.24 and 1/16→0.12 A/min example is correctly oriented. Do not choose nonlinear reactions just for larger readings.

  • Total protein: protein concentration (mg/mL) × fraction volume (mL).
  • Total activity: activity concentration (U/mL) × fraction volume (mL).
  • Recovery: 38.6/48.0 × 100% ≈ 80.4%. The manual's wording omits ×100%; include it when reporting a percentage.

For CE→60P, use the original 600 μL CE corresponding to the 40Sb pathway. Do not use the entire 2.5 mL issued CE or count 40Sa and continued 40Sb as repeated totals on one path.

Connecting Bradford concentration to these calculations

Following Experiment 2, mix 125 μL standards or unknowns with 125 μL Bradford reagent and read A595. Average replicates, subtract blank, use a consistently defined curve, and apply prior cumulative dilution.

If the x axis uses pre-reagent sample concentration in μg/mL and equal reagent ratios, do not multiply by another 2. Example: curve concentration 16.0 μg/mL with a prior 1/50 dilution gives stock 800 μg/mL = 0.800 mg/mL. Salt, elution buffer, and matrix components may interfere; check range and agreement across dilutions.

Review · Ask four questions at every step

  • Is the target in supernatant, pellet, on the column, or in eluate?
  • Are you measuring total protein, LDH activity, or PCB-containing photosensory protein?
  • Are dilution, path length, sample volume, and total fraction volume expressed in correct units?
  • Did recovery fall while specific activity rose? Is SDS-PAGE verification still needed?
