Purpose · Why study enzyme reaction rates?

Experiment 2 measured protein amount, but equal protein amounts need not have equal catalytic activity. Here alkaline phosphatase (AP) illustrates how environment and reactants affect reaction rate.

The experiment varies pH, enzyme concentration, and substrate concentration and adds inorganic phosphate as a competitive inhibitor. Determine rate, specific activity, turnover, Km, Vmax, and Ki to compare enzyme performance fairly.

Visual guide · Seeing an enzyme work

Key point 1 · Concentration and rate differ

Absorbance measures colored product at a given time; the slope of absorbance versus time measures its formation rate. Continuous assays follow the time course; fixed-time assays react for 2 minutes, stop, then measure product.

Corrected slope = (ΔA410/min)with enzyme − (ΔA410/min)without enzyme

Initial rate v₀ requires little substrate depletion, product accumulation, or reverse reaction. The manual uses approximately ≤5% substrate consumption as a practical check; substrate should greatly exceed enzyme concentration. Product divided by time estimates initial rate only when these conditions hold.

Key point 2 · From A410 to enzyme activity U

dc/dt = (dA/dt)/(εl) · 1 U = 1 μmol product/min

The manual uses ε ≈ 17,600 M⁻¹ cm⁻¹. With path length l, obtain mol/L/min, then multiply by 10⁶ and reaction volume in L for μmol/min. For microplates, use a same-plate product curve or established path length, not an assumed 1 cm.

Example A · Continuous assay and pH correction

Assume pH 8.00, enzyme slope 0.186 A/min, control slope 0.010 A/min, path length 1.00 cm, and volume 1.00 mL.

  1. Corrected slope = 0.186 − 0.010 = 0.176 A/min.
  2. Anionic-product formation rate = 0.176/(17600 × 1) = 1.00 × 10⁻⁵ mol/L/min = 10.0 μM/min.
  3. In 1.00 mL: 10.0 μmol/L/min × 0.001 L = 0.0100 μmol/min.
  4. Using pKₐ = 7.15 from Experiment 1: anion/acid = 10^(8.00 − 7.15) = 7.08. Total product rate = 0.0100 × (1 + 1/7.08) ≈ 0.0114 U.

Assume stable pH, rapid equilibration between product forms, and this pKₐ. At lower pH the same total product gives less yellow anion, so correct before comparing activity.

Key point 3 · Km, Vmax, and competitive inhibition

v₀ = Vmax[S] / (Km + [S])

[S] is substrate concentration at reaction initiation. Vmax is maximum rate at saturating substrate for the current enzyme amount and conditions; Km is substrate concentration at v₀ = Vmax/2 and is not universally equivalent to binding affinity.

Km,app = Km(1 + [I]/Ki)

Ki describes competitive inhibitor strength. Under the same model and conditions, smaller Ki generally indicates stronger inhibition. [I] is inhibitor concentration during reaction, not stop-solution concentration.

Lineweaver–Burk · Reciprocal plot used in the manual
1/v₀ = (Km/Vmax)(1/[S]) + 1/Vmax

Plot x = 1/[S], y = 1/v₀: slope m = Km/Vmax, y intercept b = 1/Vmax, x intercept = −1/Km. Therefore Vmax = 1/b,Km = m/b. Reciprocal transformation magnifies low-substrate error. This page follows the manual's method while retaining that limitation.

Teaching example: uninhibited y = 10x + 50, x in mM⁻¹ and y in U⁻¹. Vmax = 1/50 = 0.020 U,Km = 10/50 = 0.200 mM. With 0.050 mM Pi, slope = 20 and intercept = 50: Km,app = 0.400 mM; Ki = 0.050/(0.400/0.200 − 1) = 0.050 mM = 50 μM.

At [S] = 0.200 mM, uninhibited v₀ = 0.0100 U; inhibited v₀ ≈ 0.00667 U. All values are hypothetical teaching examples.

Part B · Vary enzyme amount in a 2-minute assay

  1. Table 3.3: rows A/B contain p-nitrophenol standards and blanks; row D contains enzyme stock and 1/2, 1/4, 1/8, 1/16 dilutions; rows E/F contain duplicates.
  2. Enzyme wells: 125 μL 0.1 M Tris pH 9.5 + 10 μL enzyme dilution. No-enzyme wells: 135 μL buffer.
  3. Add 35 μL 10 mM p-NPP to start; reaction volume = 170 μL and substrate concentration ≈ 2.06 mM.
  4. After exactly 2 minutes per well, add 80 μL 1 M K₂HPO₄ to stop; total volume = 250 μL; read A410.

Standards use serial 1/2 dilutions of 0.50 mM p-nitrophenol, mixing 125 μL standard + 125 μL stop solution. Include this extra 1/2 dilution in the x-axis concentration and confirm the first well's pre-mix concentration.

Standard and stopped-reaction matrices differ

The manual gives 125 μL stop solution for standards versus 80 μL for reactions. A more matched standard uses 80 μL stop solution + 45 μL water. Document the procedure actually used and acknowledge the difference.

Example B · From plate readings to specific activity

Assume the product standard x axis is final concentration after stopping, with y = 0.00400x, x in μM. Duplicate readings are 0.154 and 0.166; mean no-enzyme control = 0.040.

  1. Mean = 0.160; control-corrected = 0.120. Curve: x = 0.120/0.00400 = 30.0 μM.
  2. Use the stopped volume of 250 μL: 30 μmol/L × 0.000250 L = 0.00750 μmol.
  3. Mean 2-minute rate = 0.00750/2 = 0.00375 U.
  4. For 10 μL undiluted 0.125 mg/mL enzyme: mass = 0.010 × 0.125 = 0.00125 mg; specific activity = 0.00375/0.00125 = 3.00 U/mg.

Initial substrate = 10 mM × 35 μL = 0.350 μmol; consumption = 0.00750/0.350 ≈ 2.14%, meeting the approximate 5% check. Confirm early linearity. At high pH, nearly all product is assumed anionic.

Using the manual's 140 kDa homodimer model: enzyme = 0.00125 mg/140000 g mol⁻¹ = 8.93 × 10⁻⁶ μmol dimers. Turnover = 0.00375/(8.93 × 10⁻⁶) = 420 min⁻¹ = 7.00 s⁻¹ (per dimer). Assuming two active sites per dimer gives 3.50 s⁻¹ per site. Interpret as kcat only with saturating-substrate Vmax and established active-enzyme/site amounts.

Important checks

  • Calculate rate from the initial linear region and subtract matched no-enzyme background.
  • Correct product acid–base forms when comparing pH; do not compare A410 alone.
  • Use 170 μL for reaction substrate concentration and 250 μL for product amount from stopped concentration.
  • Keep start/stop addition order consistent and time each well for 2 minutes.
  • Specific activity is U/mg. State whether turnover is per enzyme molecule or active site.

Review questions

  • Why does darker yellow not directly establish faster activity at low pH?
  • Why might product divided by 2 minutes not represent initial rate?
  • Why does enzyme amount change Vmax while ideal competitive inhibition does not?
  • Why are post-stop substrate concentrations unsuitable for Km fitting?
