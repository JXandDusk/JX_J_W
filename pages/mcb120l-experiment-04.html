Purpose · Why copy this DNA?

Studying a protein domain often starts with obtaining its coding DNA. The target comes from the cyanobacterium Nostoc punctiforme and its NpR6012 protein, which contains four GAF domains. The fourth, GAF-4, contains 176 amino acids in the manual's selected region and can associate with a photosensory chromophore.

Amplify by PCR, remove residual reagents, measure DNA concentration, and check products with HindIII digestion and agarose electrophoresis. Primer tails prepare later directional cloning. This experiment ends with DNA analysis; ligation, transformation, and expression are background, not completed steps here.

Visual guide · The two-day workflow

Key point 1 · Three steps of a PCR cycle

StepManual settingsWhat happens?
Denaturation95°C · 30 secondsDouble-stranded DNA separates, exposing templates
Annealing55°C · 30 secondsPrimers bind matching template regions
Extension72°C · 1 minuteTaq synthesizes new DNA from primer 3′ ends

Repeat these three steps for 25 cycles (one initial cycle plus 24 more), followed by 72°C for 10 minutes and return to room temperature. Settings depend on enzyme and primers; these are the course conditions.

Ideal copy number ≈ starting templates × 2ⁿ

Teaching example: one starting template doubled ideally for 25 cycles gives 2²⁵ = 33,554,432 template-equivalent copies. Early product formation, declining efficiency, and plateau effects alter actual yields; this is not measured DNA recovery.

Key point 2 · Primer orientation and 5′ tails

Forward:5′-CC | CCATGG | GAGAAAGCTGTCACCAAG-3′
Reverse:5′-CC | CCCGGG | TTTGGCTGACTGCCCTTGT-3′

The left CC bases assist restriction digestion; the middle is NcoI (CCATGG) or SmaI (CCCGGG); the right region matches the target. A 5′ tail need not initially match the template and is later copied into products. Correct 3′ pairing governs extension.

The text says both primers have 18 target-matching bases, but the listed reverse matching region has 19 bases. Preserve the full listed sequence and verify template, vector, and original primer records rather than silently deleting a base.

Reverse complement and reading frame · Every base matters

Short teaching sequence: target end 5′-AGTC-3′ → complement 3′-TCAG-5′ → write primer 5′→3′ as GACT. Complementing without reversing gives the wrong orientation.

Expression design must preserve reading frame, start codon, domain boundaries, and Intein-CBD fusion junctions. Do not add a premature stop codon to a fusion. 176 × 3 = 528 nt estimates domain coding length; primer tails make the full PCR product longer.

Column purification · Bind → Wash → Elute

  1. Transfer 50 μL PCR product to a microtube and mix with 50 μL Binding Buffer.
  2. Apply to a GeneJet column and pass through using the course vacuum setup.
  3. Wash with 750 μL Wash Buffer and continue vacuum for approximately 2 minutes as instructed.
  4. Place the column in a clean tube labeled CLEAN PCR; add 30 μL 10 mM Tris-HCl pH 8.5 to the membrane center.
  5. Centrifuge for 1 minute as instructed, collect eluate, and refrigerate remaining product for Day 2.

Purification removes primers, nucleotides, proteins, and salts. Follow the actual product instructions for wash preparation and centrifugation; the class vacuum protocol is not universal.

DNA quantification · A260 to concentration

dsDNA concentration (ng/μL) = A260 (1 cm equivalent) × 50 × dilution factor

1 ng/μL = 1 μg/mL. NanoDrop absorbance is already normalized to a 1 cm path; do not correct twice. Use dsDNA settings and matching elution medium as blank. Record A260, A280, ratio, and instrument concentration. The manual uses 4 μL per reading.

Example · Concentration and total DNA

Assume 2 μL DNA + 8 μL elution medium gives a 1/5 dilution, with A260 = 0.120 and A280 = 0.067.

  1. Diluted concentration = 0.120 × 50 = 6.00 ng/μL.
  2. Stock concentration = 6.00 × 5 = 30.0 ng/μL.
  3. For 30 μL eluate, total DNA before sampling = 30.0 × 30 = 900 ng; after sampling, recalculate with the actual remaining volume.
  4. For 5 μL stock in digestion, DNA amount = 30.0 × 5 = 150 ng.
  5. A260/A280 = 0.120/0.067 ≈ 1.79. The ratio is supporting quality information, not proof of purity, integrity, or identity.

Other absorbers affect quantification. These are teaching values, not personal data or a measured PCR efficiency.

Incubate at 37°C for 60 minutes. The manual labels 0.5 μL HindIII as approximately 20 U; actual units depend on stock U/μL. Confirm buffer against the enzyme manufacturer's instructions.

Three restriction enzymes, different roles

HindIII(AAGCTT): Used here to generate verification fragments. NcoI (CCATGG) and SmaI (CCCGGG): Primer-tail sites for later directional insertion into pBAD/Intein-CBD. NcoI produces sticky ends and SmaI blunt ends. They are not used for vector ligation in this analysis.

Agarose gel · Smaller fragments travel farther

Negatively charged DNA migrates from wells near the cathode toward the anode. Under identical conditions, smaller linear fragments generally travel farther. Compare with known ladder sizes.

Gel preparation and loading calculations

  • 80 mL of 1.5% (w/v) gel: 1.5 g/100 mL × 80 mL = 1.20 g agarose in 1X TAE. The manual adds 4 μL 10,000X GelRed after cooling, approximately 0.5X final. Preserve the class amount rather than substituting another product recommendation.
  • Uncut: 5 μL Clean PCR + 5 μL water + 2 μL 6X loading dye = 12 μL, final dye 1X.
  • Digested: 20 μL digest + 4 μL 6X loading dye = 24 μL, final dye 1X.
  • Ladder: 5 μL premixed ladder. Manual bands of 1000, 700, 500, 200, and 100 bp correspond to 100, 70, 50, 20, and 10 ng loaded.

Each group loads uncut, digested, and ladder samples; three groups share a gel. Run at approximately 125 V until the leading orange dye reaches 70% of gel length, then image. Voltage is setup-specific. Switch power off before handling and follow imaging protection requirements.

Example · Estimate fragment length from migration

Assume 500 bp migrates 30 mm, 1000 bp migrates 20 mm, and an unknown linear fragment migrates 25 mm. Interpolate locally in log₁₀(bp) versus migration distance:

log₁₀(unknown bp) = [log₁₀500 + log₁₀1000]/2 ≈ 2.8495
Unknown size ≈ 10²·⁸⁴⁹⁵ = 707 bp.

Do not average sizes directly to 750 bp. This demonstrates local interpolation; use multiple reliable ladder bands in formal calibration. Digest fragment lengths should sum to full length, though small fragments may be invisible and similar bands may overlap.

Interpreting results

  • Main uncut band: Does its size match the complete primers and target sequence? Extra bands or smearing require investigation.
  • HindIII digest: Does the pattern match predicted cuts? Residual uncut bands may indicate incomplete digestion.
  • NTC (if included): A target-sized band suggests contamination or nonspecific amplification.
  • Limits of evidence: Size and restriction patterns support identity but cannot verify every base; sequence information or sequencing is needed for exact identity and mutation checks.
Background · Directional cloning and Gibson Assembly

Restriction cloning uses corresponding insert and vector sites, ligation, correct orientation, and reading frame. pBAD regulates expression; Intein-CBD supports later fusion processing and purification.

Gibson uses overlaps matching vector ends, followed by end processing, annealing, gap filling, and ligation. It does not automatically prevent extra amino acids: junctions, start codons, and fusion sequences still need correct design. The manual discusses it conceptually, not as a hands-on step here.

Review questions

  • Why must primer extension point into the target?
  • Why must the reverse primer be written as a reverse complement?
  • Why is 528 bp not the exact full PCR product length?
  • Why do correct concentration and band size not prove the complete sequence?
