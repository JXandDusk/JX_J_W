microRNA Screen · Sequence Analysis

Arabidopsis thaliana

Organized learning notes based on my course notebook, with recorded observations distinguished from interpretation.

Purpose

Use pooled sequencing and genetic linkage to prioritize a candidate mutation associated with a bright luciferase phenotype, then assess functional plausibility.

Skills Gained · Workplace applications

Sequencing analysis

Skill practiced: Connect read quality, alignments, BAM files, and VCF variants.

Workplace application: Assess input and output quality in a genomic workflow.

Candidate prioritization

Skill practiced: Combine linkage, variant evidence, and biological function.

Workplace application: Rank hypotheses without treating a prediction as proof.

Structural evidence assessment

Skill practiced: Interpret domain annotations and AlphaFold confidence cautiously.

Workplace application: Evaluate mechanistic hypotheses and plan validation.

Core principle

A Col-derived mutant is crossed to Ler. Bright F2 individuals are pooled so markers linked to the selected recessive phenotype are enriched for the mutant-parent background. Variant annotation and known gene function narrow the list. Linkage and plausibility nominate a candidate; they do not establish causality.

Workflow · What each stage contributes

  1. Understand how the supplied mutant families and bright F2 pools were generated.
  2. Inspect sequencing quality and align reads to the reference.
  3. Inspect alignment/coverage and call variants.
  4. Use Col/Ler marker frequencies to locate a candidate interval with SHOREmap.
  5. Annotate candidate variants and assess quality, depth, allele frequency, and coding effects.
  6. Compare gene-function, domain, and predicted-structure evidence; propose further validation.

Recorded candidate · Coordinate-to-codon reasoning

The notebook prioritizes AT1G01040 (DCL1) and records a G→A variant at chromosome 1 position 26,416. A coding-sequence position of 2,294 was interpreted as R765K:

1-based codon number = floor((2294 - 1)/3) + 1 = 765

Check reference assembly, transcript isoform, coding-strand orientation, and translated sequences before using this label. The source switches between .1 and .2 transcript labels.

Recorded observations & interpretation

The notebook identifies a chromosome 1 interval of approximately 1–429,999 bp and lists DCL1/AT1G01040 as its strongest candidate. Other listed genes include AT1G01550, AT1G01810, and AT1G01920. DCL1’s known involvement in miRNA processing makes it plausible in this reporter screen. The source’s R765K observation remains a candidate effect, not a demonstrated loss of function.

What the evidence can and cannot establish

An allele frequency of 1 is not proof of a causal mutation; quality, coverage, linkage, and other linked variants must be considered. DCL1 is a Dicer-like ribonuclease, not simply an RNA-helicase gene. Arginine-to-lysine preserves positive-charge character and does not automatically imply destabilization. AlphaFold pLDDT 70–90 indicates confident local modeling, not very-high confidence or proven mutation effects. Independent alleles, complementation/rescue, or direct functional tests would strengthen causal attribution. Stable transgenic lines, mutagenesis, and sequencing were supplied background stages rather than all independently performed in this course. Read reporter construction →

Review questions

  • Which evidence nominates DCL1, and which evidence would prove causality?
  • Why must transcript identity be checked before numbering a mutation?
