Project details · Validate the tool before trusting a screen

The reporter should change signal when miRNA regulation changes. Before interpreting bright plants as processing mutants, we need evidence that the reporter construct is correct and that the control and resistant reporter behave as expected.

First gel: PCR insert and destination vector

The insert PCR should produce a band matching the designed insert length; the no-template lane should not contain that product. The vector digest should be compared with an undigested-vector lane. A linearized backbone can migrate differently from the original circular plasmid without changing its base-pair content.

Verification gel: is the plasmid architecture plausible?

Predict fragment lengths from the final construct and the selected restriction sites before reading the image. In the notebook, one digest was interpreted as a roughly 3-kb backbone and 1-kb insert. Those are recorded estimates, not a replacement for checking the exact construct map.

An empty-vector clone can lack the expected insert fragment. Extra higher bands can arise from partial digestion or uncut plasmid; two bands are not automatically two plasmids. Co-migrating fragments and short invisible fragments can reduce the observed band count. A matching digest supports architecture but not the absence of every sequence error; sequence verification is stronger.

Read the transformation plates separately

The positive transformation control evaluates whether the competent cells and transformation procedure work. The assembly-negative control estimates background. A colony on the experimental plate is a candidate clone, not proof of the correct insert.

Read the reporter images separately

Match region size, labels, background handling, and exposure. A mutant/WT ratio above 1 indicates higher measured signal for that comparison, not automatically statistical significance or complete loss of regulation. The two own-leaf ratios are variable, so this run does not establish uniformly strong reporter discrimination.

How to read an agarose gel

Read three things separately: band position = approximate fragment size; band brightness = amount of stained DNA under comparable conditions; band number = number of resolved DNA species. None of these alone establishes a successful experiment.

  1. Start with the lane map: identify the ladder, wild-type/positive sample, experimental sample, and no-template or other negative control.
  2. Locate the wells. Negatively charged DNA travels toward the positive electrode. For comparable linear DNA fragments, shorter products generally travel farther from the wells.
  3. Compare the target band with nearby ladder bands. The spacing is not linear in base pairs; use known ladder sizes and, when needed, a calibration of log₁₀(bp) against migration distance.
  4. Compare observed products with the prediction from the primer or restriction map. A band near the expected size is consistent with the prediction but is not sequence confirmation.
  5. Check controls before interpreting missing or extra products. A failed positive control makes a negative sample result inconclusive; a target-sized band in the no-template control suggests contamination or another assay artifact.
AppearancePossible explanationsWhat to check next
Single expected-size bandLikely dominant product of the expected lengthControls, specificity, and sequence/independent verification
No bandTarget absent, PCR failed, inhibition, poor template, or loading lossPositive template control and a separate amplifiable genomic control
Extra bandsNonspecific PCR, mixed template, partial digestion, or plasmid conformationsAssay type, DNA map, reaction conditions, and controls

An undigested plasmid can produce multiple bands because supercoiled, nicked/open-circle, and other forms migrate differently. Do not estimate its size as if every band were linear DNA. In a digest, equal-length fragments can merge into one band and very short fragments may not be visible, so visible band count can differ from predicted fragment count.

Reference: NEB: additional plasmid bands · Addgene: diagnostic-digest interpretation.

Core principle

A functional miRNA/target relationship can suppress luciferase output. Comparing an intact target-site reporter with a resistant/mutant reporter tests whether signal changes are compatible with regulation. Gibson assembly joins DNA fragments with homologous overlaps; selection and diagnostic digestion are separate verification steps.

Workflow · What each stage contributes

  1. Plan the reporter construct and primers with assembly overlaps.
  2. Amplify the insert and linearize the destination vector.
  3. Clean and quantify DNA; calculate an insert:vector molar ratio.
  4. Assemble and transform, with assembly-negative and transformation-positive controls.
  5. Obtain plasmids from selected colonies and check diagnostic digests.
  6. Evaluate transient reporter behavior in tobacco leaves and quantify matched image regions.

Recorded-count example · Transformation efficiency

The positive-control plate contained 145 colonies from 9/10 of a recovery containing 5 ng of plasmid:

Estimated colonies = 145/(0.9) ≈ 161
Efficiency ≈ 161/0.005 µg ≈ 3.22 × 10⁴ CFU/µg

The 1/10 plate had 15 colonies: 15/0.1/0.005 = 3.00 × 10⁴ CFU/µg. These estimates correct for plated fraction; they are not a claim of Gibson assembly efficiency.

Assembly planning

Insert mass = vector mass × (insert length/vector length) × desired molar ratio

A 3:1 molar ratio is not necessarily a 3:1 mass ratio.

Recorded observations & interpretation

The notebook reports 8 colonies on the Gibson 9/10 plate and none on the Gibson 1/10 or assembly-negative plates. It records using a partner’s colonies for downstream work. One diagnostic digest was interpreted as approximately 3-kb and 1-kb fragments; another appeared incompletely digested. Own-leaf reporter ratios were approximately 1.234 and 1.005; a comparison group had stronger contrasts, approximately 2.48 and 2.27.

What the evidence can and cannot establish

Few colonies do not alone establish failed assembly, and incomplete digestion complicates plasmid verification. Keep partner-derived material and comparison-group imaging explicit. The own-leaf signal differences are variable and do not support an unqualified statement of robust suppression. Equal-sized regions, labeling, background, exposure, and biological replicates matter. The comparison-group notebook contains a numeric transcription discrepancy, so rounded ratios are used here. Continue to screen analysis →
