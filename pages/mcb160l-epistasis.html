Complementation & Epistasis

Saccharomyces cerevisiae

Organized learning notes based on my course notebook, with recorded observations distinguished from interpretation.

Purpose

Distinguish mutations affecting the same versus different genes, then use double-mutant phenotypes to reason about pathway relationships.

Skills Gained · Workplace applications

Replica plating

Skill practiced: Maintain colony identity across different media.

Workplace application: Compare strains without losing sample correspondence.

Genetic reasoning

Skill practiced: Separate complementation from epistasis.

Workplace application: Interpret genetic interactions and pathway experiments.

Phenotype quality control

Skill practiced: Recognize mixed-colony transfer and ambiguous patterns.

Workplace application: Identify sources of screening errors.

Core principle

For recessive mutations, mating complementary haploids can restore a functional copy of each affected gene in the diploid. Epistasis asks a different question: which phenotype is seen when both mutations are present? Tetrad analysis connects growth patterns, genotypes, and pigment phenotypes.

Workflow · What each stage contributes

  1. Follow the yeast haploid/diploid life cycle and identify the mating strains.
  2. Replica-plate the tetrad-derived colonies while preserving spatial order.
  3. Compare growth on rich and adenine-deficient media.
  4. Score colony pigment as well as growth.
  5. Use single-mutant and double-mutant phenotypes to interpret complementation and epistasis.

Conceptual example · A two-step pathway

In the course model, ade2 mutants are red and adenine-requiring; ade3 mutants are white and adenine-requiring. A white ade2 ade3 double mutant resembles ade3 for pigment. Thus ade3 is epistatic to ade2 for this readout: the upstream block prevents the red intermediate from accumulating.

This pathway interpretation depends on the defined mutants and assay conditions; epistasis is not a universal rule that the masking gene is always earlier.

Recorded observations & interpretation

The notebook interprets the white double-mutant phenotype as ade3 epistasis over ade2. It also records transferring excess colonies, creating misleading growth in the final two tetrads; the issue was discussed with the teaching assistant.

What the evidence can and cannot establish

Keep pigmentation separate from adenine-independent growth. Complementation assumptions include recessive alleles and interpretable gene functions. Genes need not be on different chromosomes for complementation; linkage affects the expected segregation patterns in tetrad analysis. The supplied plate images do not justify inventing a complete numeric tetrad dataset.

Review questions

  • What does restored diploid growth imply?
  • Why must the single-mutant phenotypes be distinguishable for epistasis?
  • How could mixed-colony transfer imitate complementation?
