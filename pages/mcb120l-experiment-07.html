UC DAVIS / MCB120L / EXPERIMENT 07

Western Blot
From DNA to protein-expression evidence

Check the BvdR insert first, then detect Strep-tagged expression products on a membrane.

Based on Experiment 7 Manual_W24 · Introductory study notes

Purpose · Does having the gene guarantee protein?

No. Plasmid DNA does not guarantee correct expression of full-length protein. Use two levels of verification:mini-prep and digestion check plasmids; Western blot checks tagged protein expression. The target is BvdR (biliverdin reductase) from cyanobacterium Synechocystis PCC6803, distinct from GAF-4 in the previous experiments.

01 · Is the DNA correct?

Compare digests of pASK75-BvdR and empty vector.

02 · Is protein expressed?

After induction, prepare whole-cell protein and detect the tag.

03 · Is the signal credible?

Interpret with empty vector, positive detection control, and total-protein gel.

Key point 1 · How is the target detected here?

Conventional Westerns often use primary and secondary antibodies; this experiment uses Strep-tag → streptavidin–alkaline phosphatase (SA-AP) → NBT/BCIP color development, not an anti-BvdR antibody protocol. SA recognizes the tag and AP develops color. AP color activity is not BvdR catalytic activity.

Distinguish avidin from streptavidin: avidin masks endogenous biotin/biotinylated proteins to reduce background, while AP-conjugated streptavidin detects Strep-tag. Tween washes reduce nonspecific adsorption.

Expression design · pASK75 and Strep-tag

pASK75 includes an origin, ampicillin resistance, tetA promoter, and TetR regulation. Anhydrotetracycline binds TetR to relieve repression. BvdR joins the tag in frame; remove its original stop codon so translation reaches the tag, then terminates at the vector's downstream stop.

Primer roles · Cloning and verification sites differ

Forward primer uses XbaI and adds RBS plus the BvdR start; reverse uses SalI and adds a C to preserve fusion frame. RBS supports translation initiation; the promoter controls transcription. The manual's wording is unclear here, so functions are distinguished explicitly.

Actual plasmid verification uses XbaI and PstI, with PstI downstream of SalI, rather than the original XbaI/SalI cloning pair. PCR and ligation are design background, not this experiment's procedures.

Day 1 · Two parallel routes

Preculture

Prepare BvdR (+) and empty-vector (−) cultures.

Expression route

Induce part of each culture for 3 hours and prepare hot-SDS whole-cell extracts.

DNA route

Mini-prep the remaining overnight culture, digest, and run agarose gels.

Induction and whole-cell samples

  1. Add 300 μL of each overnight culture to separate 2.0 mL fresh LB/amp media containing 0.2 μM anhydrotetracycline. Shake at 37°C for 3 hours. Reserve remaining overnight cultures for mini-prep.
  2. Pellet 750 μL induced culture at the microcentrifuge maximum setting for 20 seconds; discard supernatant.
  3. Resuspend cells in 75 μL water, add 75 μL 2X SDS sample buffer, mix, and heat at 100°C for 5 minutes.
  4. Centrifuge at maximum setting for 10 minutes to remove debris. Label and retain supernatant at 4°C.

75+75=150 μL gives final 1X buffer. This is whole-cell protein, not purified BvdR. The manual's 2X sample-buffer composition is incomplete; use instructor-confirmed SDS sample buffer rather than inferring a complete recipe.

Mini-prep · Lyse and isolate plasmids

  1. Pellet 1 mL overnight culture at maximum setting for 15 seconds; retain cells.
  2. Add 200 μL Solution I and fully resuspend. Tris, EDTA, and lysozyme support subsequent lysis.
  3. Add 200 μL Solution II (0.2 N NaOH, 1% SDS), gently invert.
  4. Add 200 μL Solution III (5 M potassium acetate pH 4.8), gently invert to neutralize.
  5. Centrifuge at maximum setting for 5 minutes; take 450 μL supernatant without white precipitate.
  6. Add 900 μL 95% ethanol, incubate on ice ≥10 minutes, and centrifuge at maximum setting for 5 minutes. Retain nucleic-acid pellet and remove residual liquid.
  7. Resuspend in 25 μL TE with RNase A 10 μg/mL.

Neutralization separates debris, proteins, and much genomic DNA from plasmid-containing liquid; RNase reduces RNA. Avoid vigorous mixing after lysis to limit genomic-DNA shearing. Collect bacterial and chemical waste in course-designated categories.

Restriction digestion and agarose gel

For each plasmid prepare PstI, XbaI, and double digests: six tubes. Each has 5 μL DNA + 15 μL staff-provided cocktail; incubate 37°C for 10 minutes. Add 4 μL 6X dye for 24 μL total, final dye 1X. Uncut control: 5 μL DNA + 15 μL water + 4 μL dye.

0.8% gel: 0.64 g agarose in 80 mL 1X TAE; add 4 μL GelRed after cooling as instructed. Load 10 μL DNA samples and 5 μL 1 kb ladder. Run at the class 120 V setting until leading orange dye reaches approximately 70% of gel length.

Example · Predicting double-digest bands

Hypothetical recombinant plasmid: 4.20 kb, two cuts releasing 1.05 kb; remainder = 4.20−1.05 = 3.15 kb. Fragment lengths sum to 4.20 kb; a unique-site single digest yields one 4.20 kb linear band.

These are not actual pASK75-BvdR map values. The empty-vector short fragment may be hard to see, so one visible band need not imply one site. Confirm sizes against the complete map and sequence.

Key point 2 · Transfer orientation matters

  1. Wear gloves and pencil-mark a dry PVDF corner. Wet briefly in methanol until translucent, then rinse with water and transfer buffer.
  2. Assemble wet layers as shown, gently removing bubbles at each layer, with black side toward the cathode.
  3. Use cold transfer buffer and ice packs. Transfer at the manual's 100 V for 30 minutes setting.
  4. Turn off power, remove membrane, and rinse in water. It may be dried between filter papers and refrigerated until the next class.

Transfer buffer: 25 mM Tris, 192 mM glycine, 20% methanol. Collect waste according to course requirements and preserve gel/membrane orientation and lane labels.

Day 3 · Mask background, detect, develop

  1. Rewet dry membrane in methanol for 5 seconds, rinse with water, and equilibrate 5 minutes in TBS/0.1% Tween.
  2. Replace with 15 mL HS/0.5% Tween containing avidin 2 μg/mL; rock gently for 10 minutes.
  3. Add 4 μL SA-AP conjugate directly and rock for at least 45 minutes.
  4. Wash with 30 mL each of TBS/Tween, HS/Tween, and TBS/Tween, 5 minutes per wash.
  5. Wash in 30 mL Tween-free TBS until staff-provided NBT/BCIP is ready; develop to suitable contrast.
  6. Place in water for 5 minutes, blot excess liquid, and image. Destain and image the paired Coomassie half.

The manual does not fully specify TBS or HS composition; retain names without inventing salt concentrations. Development, washing, and exposure affect background. Record visible bands before overdevelopment hides differences.

Worked examples · Dilution, amounts, and size

1 · How much detection reagent?

Avidin mass = 15 mL × 2 μg/mL = 30 μg. SA-AP ratio = 4 μL/15,000 μL, approximately 1:3750; using 15,004 μL final volume gives dilution factor ≈3751. Stock activity/concentration is unspecified, so 4 μL alone cannot give AP units.

2 · Does inoculum dilute the inducer?

If only 2.0 mL fresh medium contains 0.2 μM inducer and 0.300 mL inoculum contains none, mixed concentration = 0.2×2.0/2.3 ≈ 0.174 μM. Distinguish nominal medium concentration from concentration after mixing.

3 · Expected protein-band size

The manual's BvdR has 328 amino acids. At approximately 110 Da/residue: 328×110 ≈ 36.1 kDa. Fusion tag and linker increase mass; exact mass requires the actual full protein sequence. This is not a measured gel result.

Empty vector is a no-BvdR-gene control, not an uninduced (+) control. Both strains are induced; the manual has no before/after comparison of the same recombinant strain, so induction fold cannot be calculated directly. Positive tag control verifies detection, not BvdR activity.

Coomassie shows total protein; tag detection shows selective signal. Equal culture volumes do not guarantee equal cells or protein. Expression quantification requires loading normalization, linear detection range, and replicates. Developed band intensity does not directly give concentration.

Review questions

  • Why cannot a correct DNA digest replace protein-expression evidence?
  • Why must a C-terminal fusion avoid a stop codon before the tag?
  • Why must PVDF be on the gel's anode side?
  • Why does tag detection not establish BvdR catalytic function?
