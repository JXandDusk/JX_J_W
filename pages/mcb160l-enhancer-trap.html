Enhancer Trap & Genetic Mapping

Drosophila melanogaster

Organized learning notes based on my course notebook, with recorded observations distinguished from interpretation.

Purpose

Use a forward-genetics strategy to connect an insertion-associated phenotype to a genomic region, combining reporter observation, inheritance, recombination mapping, and sequence alignment.

Skills Gained · Workplace applications

Phenotype scoring

Skill practiced: Distinguish marker traits and record progeny classes consistently.

Workplace application: Generate reliable screening data.

Linkage analysis

Skill practiced: Infer marker order and calculate crossover intervals.

Workplace application: Localize a phenotype-associated genomic region.

Sequence-based interpretation

Skill practiced: Compare mapping with insertion-flank alignment.

Workplace application: Assess concordance between independent evidence types.

Project details · What does the screen identify?

The starting point is a phenotype and reporter pattern, rather than a chosen gene. The insertion marker tracks a P-element; reporter activity can reveal the influence of surrounding regulatory sequences. The goal is to locate the insertion-associated candidate and compare genetic and sequence evidence.

What each observation means

  • Pigmented marker eyes: help track the insertion-bearing chromosome; pigment is not the GFP tissue-expression readout.
  • GFP signal: compare the same tissue and acquisition conditions with a non-GFP control. Autofluorescence or changed exposure can imitate reporter signal.
  • Progeny marker classes: the most frequent classes usually identify parental combinations; the least frequent possible double-crossover classes help order the three loci.
  • Insertion-flanking sequence: alignment provides a physical-region candidate. It answers a different question from recombination distance.

How to interpret more or fewer flies in a class

Counts depend on sample size, recombination, viability, scoring quality, and collection. A rare class can be missed in a small sample. Use the total progeny denominator, include both reciprocal classes, and account for double crossovers in each adjacent interval.

A cM interval measures recombination behavior; it is not a fixed number of base pairs. A disagreement between mapping and alignment calls for checking phenotype scoring, marker order, calculations, and sequence quality. The project’s principal readouts are microscopy, progeny counts, and sequence alignment, rather than a knockout-verification gel.

What would strengthen the candidate-gene claim?

Concordant mapping and insertion location nominate a gene. Independent alleles, rescue, or demonstrating the relevant effect of the insertion would provide stronger causal evidence. A nearby gene is not automatically the cause.

Core principle

A P-element reporter can respond to nearby regulatory activity. The marker allows insertion-bearing flies to be followed through crosses. Parental and double-crossover classes help infer gene order; flanking sequence provides a separate route to a physical locus.

Workflow · What each stage contributes

  1. Recognize sex and marker phenotypes under a microscope.
  2. Compare GFP-positive samples with a non-GFP/background control.
  3. Set up the crosses and score marker combinations in progeny.
  4. Identify parental, single-crossover, and double-crossover classes.
  5. Calculate adjacent map intervals and compare with the course reference map.
  6. Align provided insertion-flanking sequence and evaluate candidate-gene evidence.

The notebook lists parental counts 347 and 437; double crossovers 33 and 44; two single-crossover pairs 119/112 and 60/41; total 1,193. Comparing the genotypes places P between lz and f.

lz–P: (119 + 112 + 33 + 44)/1193 × 100 ≈ 25.8 cM
P–f: (60 + 41 + 33 + 44)/1193 × 100 ≈ 14.9 cM

The observed adjacent intervals sum to approximately 40.7 cM. If both are proportionally scaled to the course reference lz–f span of 29 cM, P is approximately 18.4 cM right of lz: 27.7 + 18.4 ≈ 46.1 cM. This is an explicit reanalysis of the tabulated class counts, not the final position written in the original notebook.

Recorded observations & interpretation

The individual sample (131 flies) and class sample (1,193 flies) led to different order interpretations in the notebook. The class genotypes support the order lz–P–f. The original written calculations report several incompatible final positions, so they should not be carried forward as one settled result.

What the evidence can and cannot establish

Three-point map intervals count double crossovers in both adjacent intervals. The 25.8-cM interval should not be labeled the full lz–f span when P is the middle marker. The reference scaling above is a course normalization, not a general mapping function. Genetic distance and base-pair coordinates are distinct; an insertion near a gene is a candidate association, not automatic proof of causality.

Review questions

  • Why are double crossovers useful for ordering markers?
  • Why can small samples produce an unstable map?
  • How can sequence alignment complement genetic mapping?
