Purpose · Why run proteins on a gel?

Experiment 5 spectra support chromophore-containing GAF-4, but other proteins may remain. Here sodium dodecyl sulfate–polyacrylamide gel electrophoresis (SDS-PAGE) separates proteins mainly by size, tests whether contaminant bands decrease, and estimates apparent subunit molecular mass from migration.

01 · Separate proteins

SDS unfolds proteins and gives substantial negative charge; the gel sieves them, so smaller proteins generally migrate farther.

02 · Estimate size

Measure band migration against molecular-mass standards on the same gel.

03 · Check purification

Compare crude extract, ammonium-sulfate fraction, and affinity eluate while accounting for protein loading.

Key point 1 · Roles of SDS, reducing agent, and heat

SDS: An anionic detergent whose hydrophobic tail binds proteins and sulfate group carries negative charge. Most proteins bind approximately 1.4 g SDS/g protein, giving similar charge-to-mass ratios. Heat: Promotes denaturation. 2-mercaptoethanol: Reduces disulfide bonds so disulfide-linked chains separate.

Shape and charge differences are reduced, so separation mainly reflects polypeptide size. SDS-PAGE reports apparent subunit molecular mass, not native complex size, and does not preserve original enzyme activity.

Example · One band need not mean one subunit

Manual LDH: native tetramer ≈140 kDa, with four similar ≈35 kDa chains. Denaturation may produce one 35 kDa band because identical-size chains comigrate. Higher-plant Rubisco has large and small subunits, giving approximately 56 and 12 kDa bands. Band types do not directly count subunits.

Key point 2 · Stack first, resolve second

In the stacking region, leading chloride and slower glycine create a moving ion boundary that concentrates proteins into a narrow zone. In the higher-pH, smaller-pore resolving gel, glycine accelerates and proteins separate by size.

The manual describes stacking pH ≈6.8 and resolving pH ≈8.8–9.0. These are system principles, not universal precast formulations. Acrylamide and bis-acrylamide form a crosslinked gel, with APS/TEMED initiating polymerization; this experiment uses precast gels.

Native PAGE versus SDS-PAGE

Native PAGE preserves structure as far as possible; migration depends jointly on charge, size, and shape, so distance alone does not determine mass. SDS-PAGE approximately standardizes charge/mass and estimates polypeptide size by sieving. Physical mobility μ = v/E has units cm²/(V·s); Rm below is a dimensionless distance ratio, not the same quantity.

Procedure overview · Continuing Experiment 5

  1. Two groups share one 12%, 10-well precast gel. Follow instructor guidance to remove bottom tape and comb and assemble the tank. Add running buffer, check the upper chamber for leaks, then fill the lower chamber.
  2. Reheat prepared Experiment 5 SDS samples at 100°C for 2 minutes, then cool to room temperature. Standards are already prepared; do not reformulate them.
  3. Load according to the table and record lane, sample, and volume. Avoid puncturing wells, overflow, or cross-contamination.
  4. Use the course apparatus at constant 200 V for approximately 45 minutes; stop when the dye front is about 0.5 cm from the bottom. Turn off power before disassembly.
  5. Handle fragile gels gently, separate plates, and transfer to a container. Preserve lane orientation for staining and imaging.

Worked example 2 · Estimate molecular mass from migration

Rm = protein migration distance / dye-front distance

Measure both from the same origin at the resolving-gel top; use band centers. Record the dye front before staining, when it may become less visible. Larger Rm generally indicates smaller proteins.

Unknown band at 34.0 mm · Step by step

  1. Unknown Rm = 34.0/50.0 = 0.680, between the 29.0 and 18.4 kDa standards.
  2. Local interpolation t = (0.680−0.600)/(0.760−0.600) = 0.500.
  3. log₁₀(MW/kDa) = log₁₀29.0 + 0.500×(log₁₀18.4−log₁₀29.0) ≈ 1.3636.
  4. MW ≈ 10¹·³⁶³⁶ = 23.1 kDa.

Do not directly average (29.0+18.4)/2 = 23.7 kDa. Interpolate in log molecular mass. Formal analysis should fit multiple reliable standards and report an apparent mass. This unknown is neither a predicted nor measured GAF-4 result.

How large should GAF-4 be?

Experiment 4 defines a 176-amino-acid domain. Using approximately 110 Da per residue: 176×110 ≈ 19.4 kDa. This is a rough domain estimate, not a substitute for the full actual product sequence. Linker residues, cleavage boundaries, and retained Intein-CBD alter mass. Released domain and earlier fusion should not have identical sizes.

Staining · Observe first, then reveal blue bands

  1. Zinc stain: Cover with 20 mM zinc acetate / 150 mM Tris pH 7.0, rock gently at room temperature for 10 minutes, and photograph using the UV-P imager as instructed.
  2. Coomassie: Manual stain: 0.1% Coomassie Blue R-250, 45% methanol, 10% acetic acid. Cover the gel until the next class.
  3. Destain: Rinse 2–3 times with deionized water and add 10% (v/v) acetic acid. Place Kimwipes in a corner to absorb background dye without contacting gel. Rock gently, replace every 30 minutes, and continue as needed after approximately 30–60 minutes.
  4. Image: Once background and contrast are suitable, photograph on a lightbox or use the manual's Li-COR 700 nm channel with 1-minute exposure. Label lanes, samples, standard sizes, and protein loading.

R-250 stains gels; Experiment 2 Bradford uses G-250. Color roughly reflects mass, but protein-specific response and image saturation prevent precise purity estimates by eye. Collect methanol, dye, and acid waste according to current course rules rather than treating old sink-disposal wording as universal.

Interpreting results

  • Fewer contaminant bands: Support purification, subject to loading and staining detection limits.
  • Main band size: Does it match the actual expressed, cleaved product sequence? Similar size alone does not identify protein; later Western evidence may help.
  • Extra high-mass bands: Could be retained fusion, aggregates, or incomplete reduction/denaturation; a gel alone does not establish the cause.
  • Low-mass bands: May include degradation products or other proteins.
  • Smears or broad bands: Check overloading, salts/matrix, well damage, denaturation, and running conditions.
  • One visible band: Supports greater homogeneity, not absolute purity. Proteins can comigrate and low-abundance contaminants may escape staining.
Why can apparent size be inaccurate?

Glycosylation, unusual SDS binding, hydrophobicity, and unreduced disulfides alter migration. Do not state a precise linear 1/MW relation. Calibration applies to this gel and conditions; extrapolation beyond range is unreliable.

Review questions

  • Why is SDS-PAGE mainly size-based while Native PAGE is not?
  • Why does 20 μL prepared sample equal only 10 μL original fraction?
  • Why calibrate unknown size in log(MW)?
  • Why does one 35 kDa band not prove native LDH has one subunit?
