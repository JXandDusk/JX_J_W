Project details · Why amplify the repeat locus?

A human genome contains too much unrelated DNA for this particular repeat array to be read directly from a total-DNA gel. Primers flanking D1S80 enrich a defined target, so PCR product length can report repeat-array length.

Reading the two assays

TH01 is a separate control amplification in this course; compare it with its own expected range. D1S80 products have a different expected size relationship. Do not treat a TH01 band as a third D1S80 allele.

  • Two clear D1S80 bands: compatible with two resolved repeat-length alleles.
  • One clear band: compatible with same-length alleles, but inadequate resolution or allele dropout must be considered.
  • No target band: check DNA quality, amplification conditions, and control behavior before making a genotype statement.
  • More than two bands: investigate artifacts, nonspecific products, or mixed samples; the band count alone does not establish extra copies of the locus.

Why a 50-bp estimate is not exact genotyping

Alleles differ by 16 bp in the course model. A rough measurement to the nearest 50 bp is too coarse to distinguish every adjacent repeat allele. Use it for an approximate repeat estimate and communicate the uncertainty.

What does a larger band mean?

For the same correctly amplified locus, a larger linear product is compatible with more repeat units. A brighter band instead reflects more stained product under the imaging conditions; it does not mean more repeats. Neither measurement determines ethnicity or identifies an individual on its own.

How to read an agarose gel

Read three things separately: band position = approximate fragment size; band brightness = amount of stained DNA under comparable conditions; band number = number of resolved DNA species. None of these alone establishes a successful experiment.

  1. Start with the lane map: identify the ladder, wild-type/positive sample, experimental sample, and no-template or other negative control.
  2. Locate the wells. Negatively charged DNA travels toward the positive electrode. For comparable linear DNA fragments, shorter products generally travel farther from the wells.
  3. Compare the target band with nearby ladder bands. The spacing is not linear in base pairs; use known ladder sizes and, when needed, a calibration of log₁₀(bp) against migration distance.
  4. Compare observed products with the prediction from the primer or restriction map. A band near the expected size is consistent with the prediction but is not sequence confirmation.
  5. Check controls before interpreting missing or extra products. A failed positive control makes a negative sample result inconclusive; a target-sized band in the no-template control suggests contamination or another assay artifact.

An undigested plasmid can produce multiple bands because supercoiled, nicked/open-circle, and other forms migrate differently. Do not estimate its size as if every band were linear DNA. In a digest, equal-length fragments can merge into one band and very short fragments may not be visible, so visible band count can differ from predicted fragment count.

Reference: NEB: additional plasmid bands · Addgene: diagnostic-digest interpretation.

Core principle

Primers bind outside a variable repeat array. Product length includes a constant flanking segment plus a repeat-dependent segment. A diploid sample may show one or two principal allele bands, subject to resolution, amplification quality, and artifacts.

Workflow · What each stage contributes

  1. Obtain and prepare a cheek-cell DNA sample.
  2. Amplify the variable D1S80 locus and use TH01 as a separate assay control.
  3. Compare PCR bands with a DNA size ladder.
  4. Estimate repeat number from the product length and flanking length.
  5. Discuss uncertainty, unresolved alleles, primer dimers, and control behavior.

Teaching example · Repeat-number calculation

The course D1S80 model has 130 + 32 = 162 bp of flanking sequence and 16 bp per repeat:

Product length = 162 + 16n
For a 530-bp product: n = (530 - 162)/16 = 23 repeats

A rough size estimate giving a noninteger n should prompt consideration of measurement uncertainty. It does not indicate a fractional biological repeat.

Recorded observations & interpretation

This page focuses on the assay and its interpretation rather than publishing a personal cheek-cell allele profile. The notebook used a variable-locus gel and a TH01 control to discuss PCR products. Its allele-to-population interpretation is not a supported conclusion from this assay.

What the evidence can and cannot establish

Use 16 bp per repeat, not 160. A single VNTR locus cannot determine an individual’s ethnicity or assign one allele a nationality. Population-frequency tables describe groups and are not ancestry labels. One visible band may be compatible with homozygosity, but unresolved alleles or allele dropout can also occur. A successful TH01 control does not eliminate every D1S80-specific assay problem.
